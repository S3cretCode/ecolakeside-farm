The Planning Mandate

Key Limitations & Watershed Challenges

Our 240-hectare farmland has rich potential, but also four major physical limitations that demand smart agricultural design:

  • Sandy Loam Soil (pH 6.0–7.0): Sandy soil is loose and easy for root expansion, but drains water rapidly and holds few nutrients. Without precautions, dry spells cause drought stress, while heavy rains can wash nitrogen straight down toward groundwater.
  • Eastern Downward Slope: The land slopes directly toward a freshwater lake on the east. Rain easily picks up speed downhill, carrying loose topsoil and fertilizer directly toward the lake.
  • Drought & Moisture Vulnerability: Because water perks through sandy loam so fast, summer dry spells can quickly deplete crop root moisture without active retention strategies.
  • Sensitive Freshwater Lake: Excess nitrogen and phosphorus runoff triggers toxic blue-green algae blooms, depleting oxygen and harming fish. Our plan guarantees zero unfiltered runoff reaches the lake.

Our Solution: By pairing Potatoes with Garlic in a companion planting system, farming perpendicular to the slope (across contours) to stop runoff, and routing drainage through a 3-stage biological water-cleaning system, we protect the lake while maximizing farm profitability.

Environmental Defense & Circular Water

Soil Dynamics & Lake Protection Plan

How we keep our soil fertile, stop erosion, biologically clean our water supply, and protect the lake with natural defenses.

Soil Health & Moisture

Sandy Loam Care & Drought Defense

Perpendicular Farming Across the Slope

Rather than tilling downhill toward the lake, our tractor passes run strictly across the slope (perpendicular to water flow). Each raised furrow acts like a miniature dam, catching rainfall where it falls, stopping fast runoff, and keeping fertile topsoil on the field.

Drought Precautions & Biochar Sponge

Coarse sandy soil dries out quickly during prairie summers. We add loamy biochar and composted organic matter to dramatically boost the soil’s water-holding capacity, keeping root zones moist for days longer during dry spells.

Sustainable Manure Used Away from Water

We recycle composted manure from regional livestock farms to feed soil biology and build humus naturally. Crucially, manure is applied only to upper inland zones, strictly 150+ meters away from the riparian lake buffer, so no animal waste or nutrients ever touch the water.

Winter Cover Crops to Lock Soil

Right after our potato and garlic harvests, we plant cold-hardy winter rye. The rye's thick roots bind loose sand throughout freezing winters and spring thaws, stopping wind and meltwater erosion completely.

Water Quality & Ecosystem

Cleaning Water & Natural Biological Allies

Cleaning the Water Supply (3-Stage Filtration)

Field runoff doesn't reach the lake. Instead, it enters a natural cleaning circuit: settling out silt in a sediment trap, filtering through wetland cattails and bulrushes that absorb dissolved nitrogen and phosphorus, and storing crystal-clean polished water in our retention pond.

Closed-Loop Irrigation (Zero Direct Lake Draw)

Our precision drip and pivot irrigation systems draw 100% of their water from our recycled retention ponds. We take zero water from the natural lake, protecting fish spawning beds and shoreline water levels.

Bumblebee Presence & Pollination Benefits

Our 30-meter tree buffer and wildflower strips create permanent homes for native wild bumblebees (Bombus). Bumblebees provide specialized "buzz pollination" (vibrating blossoms with wing muscles), improving potato flowering vigor and keeping the local ecosystem healthy and resilient.

Natural Predators Stop Colorado Potato Beetles

Instead of spraying harmful insecticides, we let nature work for us. Wildflower borders shelter ladybugs, green lacewings, predatory stink bugs, and ground beetles that eagerly eat Colorado potato beetle eggs and larvae, keeping crops healthy without toxic chemicals.

Precision Nutrient Management

Stopping Leaching & Groundwater Contamination

Subsurface Banding

Fertilizer is placed in tight bands 2 inches beside and below seeds, right where roots can drink it. No surface broadcasting that could wash away in rainfall or blow away in the wind.

Controlled-Release Nutrients

We use polymer-coated granules that slowly feed nitrogen as soil warms, matching crop growth speed so excess fertilizer never sits loose in sandy soil.

Groundwater Protection

Split feeding during peak tuber growth and deep-rooted cover crops prevent nitrate leaching, keeping the underground drinking water table 100% pure and safe.

How Our 3-Stage Ecological Water Filtration Cleans Runoff

Stage 01: Sedimentation Basin

Gravity naturally settles out sand, silt, and loose soil particles before water moves forward.

75%+ Suspended Soil Trapped
Stage 02: Wetland Bio-Filter

Native cattails, rushes, and root microbes naturally consume and filter out dissolved nitrates and phosphorus.

88%+ Dissolved Nutrients Absorbed
Stage 03: Polishing & Drip Reuse

Purified, clean water rests in our retention basin, ready to be pumped back through high-efficiency crop drip lines.

100% Recirculated • Zero Lake Extraction

Agronomic Defense

Crop Selection Rationale: Potatoes & Garlic

Why pairing Potatoes and Garlic is the ultimate companion cropping strategy for sandy loam, pest defense, and high-value yield.

Russet & Table Potatoes 100 ha (41.7%)
Topographic Placement: Upper Western Slope

Potatoes hate waterlogged soil, which causes tuber rot and late blight. Placing them on the highest ground ensures gentle gravity drainage through the sandy loam.

Soil Compatibility: Sandy Loam (pH 6.0–6.5)

Loose sandy loam gives tubers room to expand into uniform, high-grade potatoes without physical constriction. Keeping soil pH below 6.5 naturally stops potato scab disease.

High Food Energy Density

Potatoes generate up to 15 million calories per hectare, packed with vitamin C, potassium, and healthy carbohydrates to feed thousands of families reliably.

Hardneck Garlic (Companion) 110 ha (45.8%)
Natural Pest Shield Against Colorado Beetles

As verified by Gilmour's companion planting guide, garlic's strong sulfur scent naturally repels Colorado potato beetles, aphids, and flea beetles, safeguarding adjacent potato rows without synthetic chemicals.

Non-Competing Root Zones

Garlic has shallow, compact root systems that do not compete with potato tubers for moisture or soil space. Garlic also releases natural sulfur compounds that suppress fungal root rot.

Mid-Summer Harvest & Cover Timing

Garlic is harvested in late July to early August, weeks before potatoes. This staggered schedule allows immediate summer cover cropping with buckwheat or clover, rebuilding soil organic matter.

Three-Year Companion Crop Rotation Cycle

Year 1: Potatoes + Winter Rye

High-yield potato harvest followed immediately by drilled winter rye to anchor topsoil through freezing prairie winters.

Year 2: Garlic + Summer Green Manure

Garlic planted in autumn and harvested mid-summer. Followed immediately by nitrogen-fixing clover and buckwheat to restore soil health.

Year 3: Legume / Brassica Bio-Fumigant

Peas, alfalfa, or mustard crop fixes atmospheric nitrogen, breaks all potato pathogen cycles, and revitalizes beneficial soil microbes.

With our companion crops working together in the fields, we examine how this harvest supports long-term food and water security.

Sovereignty & Resilience

Food & Water Security Framework

Delivering high-energy staple nutrition, high-value specialty crops, and a 100% closed-loop clean water supply.

Caloric & Market Security

Potatoes & Garlic Food Architecture

High Caloric Energy (15M Cal/ha): Potatoes yield up to 15 million calories per hectare, significantly outproducing cereal grains in usable food energy, dietary fiber, vitamin C, and potassium per litre of water applied.

High-Value Specialty Garlic Market: Garlic sells at $8 to $14 per kilogram at farmgate, creating strong revenue diversity and buffering our farm against fluctuating potato commodity prices.

Long Shelf-Life Food Reserves: Both cured garlic and temperature-managed potatoes store for 6 to 9 months without processing, offering stable year-round regional food security across Manitoba and western Canada.

Water Circularity

Closed-Loop Water & Zero Lake Depletion

180,000 m³ Stormwater & Melt Capture: Our retention ponds capture spring snowmelt and summer thunderstorms, storing ample clean water for the entire growing season.

Zero Water Pumped from Lake: By recycling on-farm runoff, we draw zero water from the adjacent lake. Shoreline fish habitats, native wetland birds, and lake water levels remain untouched.

High-Efficiency Drip Irrigation: Drip lines deliver water directly beneath crop roots at 95% efficiency, completely avoiding the 30%+ evaporative loss common with traditional overhead spray guns.

Nutrients For Life Canada • Standards Framework

4R Nutrient Stewardship Certification

EcoLakeside Farm adheres strictly to Fertilizer Canada’s science-based 4R Nutrient Stewardship framework, supported by Nutrients for Life Canada. By aligning nutrient inputs with potato and garlic biology, we maximize yield while eliminating nutrient loss to the watershed.

A Canadian Foundation • Visit Website →
Pillar 01

Right Source

Polymer-coated controlled-release urea (ESN) releases nitrogen gradually as soil warms. Composted manure applied strictly to upper inland fields feeds soil biology sustainably without runoff risk.

Pillar 02

Right Rate

Precision GPS soil sampling calibrated specifically to potato tuber demand and garlic sulfur requirements. Variable-Rate Application prevents over-fertilizing sandy ridges.

Pillar 03

Right Time

Split feeding: starter at planting and fertigation during active tuber bulbing. Strict zero application on frozen, snow-covered, or waterlogged ground.

Pillar 04

Right Place

Subsurface precision banding 2 inches below and beside seeds. Zero surface broadcasting near the 30-meter riparian lake buffer or water channels.
